What 3,546 Texas House votes look like

Every recorded vote of the 2025 session, drawn three ways: how far apart the two parties were, which members broke with their own party, and where those members are.

Most votes were not party-line

Each column counts roll calls by the gap between the share of Republicans and the share of Democrats who voted yes. On 1,634 of the 3,546 (46%), the two parties were within 10 points of each other, and 932 were unanimous. On 136 (4%), they were 80 points or more apart.

The source does not separate final passage from amendments and procedural motions, so every recorded vote is counted, and routine votes are part of why so many are nearly unanimous.

Who breaks with their own party

One dot per member, placed at the share of contested votes, the 649 where most Republicans and most Democrats voted opposite ways, on which that member voted against the majority of their own party. The median Democrat did it on 8% of them and the median Republican on 18%. The three Democrats furthest right are Richard Raymond (HD-42), Terry Canales (HD-40) and Sergio Muñoz (HD-36), all from the border region. The three Republicans are Ken King (HD-88), Ryan Guillen (HD-31) and Charlie Geren (HD-99).

Dustin Burrows (District 83), the Speaker, cast none of these votes and is left out.
