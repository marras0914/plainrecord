Vikki Goodwin and Dan Patrick on the 2025 Texas bills

They are running for Lieutenant Governor of Texas. Only one of them has cast a vote on the bills this site asks about, and that is a fact about the offices they have held rather than a judgement about either of them. This page says exactly what the record shows for each, and where the two kinds of record stop being comparable.

What Vikki Goodwin's record shows

Vikki Goodwin sits in the Texas House, so there is a roll call on these bills. Of the 67 votes this site asks about, Vikki Goodwin cast 64. On the 49 of those where the two parties took opposite sides, they voted against their own party's majority 2 times.

Where they broke with their own party

  • HB 5111 — Sets who may serve as a campaign treasurer: at least 18, a Texas resident, not already treasurer for someone else, and not convicted of certain election offences.
  • HB 3179 — Lets the county where the Llano and James rivers meet charge its own hotel tax, except at hotels a city already taxes.

What Dan Patrick's record shows

Why there are no votes. Presides over the Senate rather than voting in the House, so he casts no vote on these bills.

What there is instead. Priority-bill designations — bills he publicly named as must-pass.

What that evidence cannot tell you. Every bill on that list is one he wanted passed, so it can show where he stood on a bill but never how often he would agree with you.

Why these are not the same kind of evidence

A vote is a choice between yes and no on one question, made at the same moment as 149 other people, and it can go either way. A signature is the last word on a bill that already passed, and a priority list names only bills somebody wanted. Printing them side by side as though they were one measurement would flatter whichever candidate you already preferred, so this page keeps them apart and says what each one is.
