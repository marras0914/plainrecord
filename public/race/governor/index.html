Gina Hinojosa and Greg Abbott on the 2025 Texas bills

They are running for Governor of Texas. Only one of them has cast a vote on the bills this site asks about, and that is a fact about the offices they have held rather than a judgement about either of them. This page says exactly what the record shows for each, and where the two kinds of record stop being comparable.

What Gina Hinojosa's record shows

Gina Hinojosa sits in the Texas House, so there is a roll call on these bills. Of the 67 votes this site asks about, Gina Hinojosa cast 62. On the 48 of those where the two parties took opposite sides, they voted against their own party's majority 2 times.

Where they broke with their own party

  • SB 1498 — Lets the state seize cryptocurrency through civil forfeiture, by adding digital currency to the property that can be taken as suspected proceeds of a crime.
  • HB 5111 — Sets who may serve as a campaign treasurer: at least 18, a Texas resident, not already treasurer for someone else, and not convicted of certain election offences.

What Greg Abbott's record shows

Why there are no votes. Has never served in a legislature, so he has no roll-call record at all.

What there is instead. What he did with each bill that reached him — signed it, vetoed it, or let it become law without signing.

What that evidence cannot tell you. Every bill that reaches a governor gets one of three answers, so unlike a priority list this can show both directions. It is still uneven: he signed 26 of the 31 that reached him, so a signature mostly means he did not object rather than that he pushed. The 4 he let become law without signing are recorded as taking no side, because that is what they are. 34 of these bills never reached his desk at all, and he appears on none of those.

Why these are not the same kind of evidence

A vote is a choice between yes and no on one question, made at the same moment as 149 other people, and it can go either way. A signature is the last word on a bill that already passed, and a priority list names only bills somebody wanted. Printing them side by side as though they were one measurement would flatter whichever candidate you already preferred, so this page keeps them apart and says what each one is.
