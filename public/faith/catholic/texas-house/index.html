The Texas House and the Catholic bishops’ bills, 2025

On each bill the Texas Catholic bishops supported or opposed in the 2025 session that passed the Texas House by recorded vote: how each party voted.

Not yet reviewed. No one from the traditions on these pages has reviewed what they say. Every summary is this site's own, and each one links to the full text so you can check it.

Entries reviewed so far: 0 of 64.

The positions are the bishops’ own, from the Texas Catholic Conference of Bishops’ two bill-position pages and its end-of-session report, as they read on November 13, 2026. This site does not decide which way Catholic teaching points on any bill. The bishops add:

“The TCCB does not take positions on all bills in any legislative session. The analysis of the bills is made at various phases of the legislative process and may not accurately reflect the final status of the bill.”

The count

Of the 579 bills the bishops took a side on, 88 passed the House by a recorded vote that this site has member by member. The rest are listed further down. On those 88:

PartyVoted the bishops’ wayVoted the other wayEvenly split
Republicans74 (84%)122
Democrats73 (83%)150

A party voted the bishops’ way when most of its members who voted yea or nay did what the bishops asked: yea on a bill they supported, nay on one they opposed. Members who did not vote are left out.

The two parties’ majorities voted differently on 23 of the 88 bills. On the rest, both voted the same way.

This count covers one church’s positions and one chamber’s final votes. It is not added to anything else, and it does not measure which party is more Catholic or more Christian. The bishops’ list holds the bills they chose to weigh in on, and a final-passage vote is on the bill as amended, which the bishops may have judged at an earlier stage.

How this was put together

  1. Which bills. Every bill the bishops mark as supported or opposed on either bill-position page, and every bill in their end-of-session report whose position follows from the report’s own definitions: a win is a supported bill passing or an opposed bill failing, a loss the reverse. The bill number is the one the bishops wrote.
  2. Which vote. The House’s recorded vote on final passage (third reading), as the bill’s history on Texas Legislature Online lists it. Not amendments, not procedural motions, and not later votes to accept the Senate’s changes.
  3. Who voted how. Each member’s vote comes from the House Journal, through the same file the rest of this site uses: every Texas House vote of 2025. On a few bills the two parties’ counts add up to one fewer than the official total, because the source misspells one member’s name on that vote, so the file records the vote but cannot say whose it was. Only the House: this site does not yet have the Senate’s votes.
