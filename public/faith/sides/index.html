Which side each tradition takes

Where the Republican and Democratic platforms take opposite sides on a point, this page shows which side each tradition's official statements take, in the tradition's own words. The site picks neither the question nor the answers: the platforms set both.

How these pages are checked. Every summary is this site’s own; no one from the traditions reviews them. A program checks every quote against a saved copy of its source, every summary links to the full text, and every placement follows the rules on the methodology page. A better source corrects anything here.

Criminal justice

The platforms address different things: the Texas Republican platform bail and uniform enforcement, the Texas Democratic platform addiction, homelessness and bias, the national platforms police funding and private prisons. None takes the opposite side of another on one point, so no church is placed.

How a tradition is placed

  1. The point on each issue is one where the Republican and Democratic platforms take opposite sides in their own words. An issue with no such point is not compared.
  2. A tradition is placed on a side only if one of the official statements on its page takes that side in its own words, and that quote is shown. Otherwise it is marked “Doesn't say”.
  3. Every quote is word for word, and a program checks each one against the downloaded source.
  4. No count across issues, here or in the data: which issues are on the list would decide any total.

Three judgment calls

Three placements needed a judgment about what a statement's words cover. They are listed so you can disagree with them.

  • Southern Baptist, environment: its 2007 resolution supports only “cost-effective measures” to reduce emissions and asks Congress to “reject government-mandated reductions”. It is placed with opposing climate measures.
  • United Methodist, healthcare: it calls on governments to ensure that every human being has access to medical services, but never names Medicaid. It is placed with widening public coverage.
  • Catholic, environment: the bishops call for responding to climate change “with actions”, in a document on Catholics' political responsibility. It is placed with government action.
