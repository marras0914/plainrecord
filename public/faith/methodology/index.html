How the faith pages were made

These pages report what five Christian traditions have officially said and what the parties' platforms say. They never decide what "the Christian position" is.

Not yet reviewed. No one from the traditions on these pages has reviewed what they say. Every summary is this site's own, and each one links to the full text so you can check it.

Not yet reviewed

The plan was to publish only after people from several traditions, from conservative and progressive congregations, had checked that their own tradition is described fairly. Reviewers have not been found yet, and the pages are published without them, saying so on every page. Reviewers will be named here as they sign off, unless they ask not to be.

A correction is made the same way as any other on this site: a change to the public record, with a better source. The record and its history are on GitHub.

The rules

  1. Every entry links to a public source and gives its date.
  2. One fixed list of eight issues, and every tradition and both parties, in Texas and nationally, are placed on every one.
  3. A position is reported only where the source states one. Mentioning an issue is not taking a position on it.
  4. "No official statement" is shown as an entry, not left blank, and so is a platform that says nothing on an issue.
  5. Quotes are word for word and under 15 words. Summaries describe and do not characterize: every plank summary starts with the same verb, and none uses words like "extreme" or "common sense" in the site's own voice.
  6. Each tradition's current statement on an issue is shown in full, and every earlier one is listed by title, date and link, so a position that has moved shows the movement.

What counts as official, tradition by tradition

The rule is fixed per tradition and is the same for every issue: the body that speaks for the tradition, not its staff, its agencies or its leaders' columns.

  • Catholic Church in the United States: Statements approved by the full body of U.S. bishops (USCCB), Forming Consciences for Faithful Citizenship first. Not staff, committee or diocesan letters.
  • Southern Baptist Convention: Resolutions adopted by the messengers at an SBC Annual Meeting. Not entity or agency statements.
  • The United Methodist Church: The Social Principles and the Book of Resolutions, as adopted by the General Conference (2024 revision). The Social Principles are shown in full where they address the issue; otherwise the Book of Resolutions entry on it.
  • African Methodist Episcopal Church: General Conference resolutions, then statements of the Council of Bishops.
  • Assemblies of God (USA): Position papers and official statements of the General Council or its Executive Presbytery. Not columns by its officers.

Why these five

One from each of five large families of churches: Catholic, Baptist, mainline Protestant, historically Black and Pentecostal. The United Methodist Church was chosen over the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America because it is larger in Texas and revised its Social Principles in 2024; the African Methodist Episcopal Church over the National Baptist Convention because it publishes more formal positions.

How earlier statements are found

For the Southern Baptist Convention, all 1,257 of its resolutions are downloaded from its own library, and a resolution is listed under an issue when its title contains one of a fixed set of words for that issue. The rule is mechanical, so the list is the same whoever builds it; its cost is that a resolution about an issue that does not name it in its title is not listed. The words for each issue are in the downloadable record. For the other traditions, earlier statements are listed by hand.

Bible passages

The site picks no verses. A passage appears only where a statement on these pages cites it, found by a program that reads each statement's own text. A document about one issue counts whole. In a document that covers several issues, such as the bishops' Forming Consciences, only passages cited in the same section as the quoted excerpt count, so a passage the bishops cite on the Church's role in public life is not listed under any one issue. Passages of more than eight verses, and whole chapters, are linked rather than printed.

References are shown as the churches wrote them. The Southern Baptist resolution of 2006 cites Genesis 1:32, a verse that does not exist (the chapter has 31), and the page says so rather than correcting it.

Two references were garbled when their text was taken from a PDF, and are shown as the printed page has them: Eph. 4:2947 32 → Eph. 4:29-32; Lk 6:3638 → Lk 6:36-38; Luke 10:30– 37 → Luke 10:30-37.

The same program finds no Bible reference in any of the four platforms.

Verse text: the World English Bible in English and the Reina-Valera 1909 in Spanish, both public domain, from eBible.org.

Votes on bills a church took a side on

Where a church has itself said it supports or opposes a specific Texas bill, a page shows how each party in the Texas House voted on that bill when it passed. The site never decides which way a church’s teaching points on a bill. Of the five churches, only the Texas Catholic bishops publish such a list, so there is one such page: The Texas House and the Catholic bishops’ bills, 2025. Its count is for that one church and is not added to anything else.

How every quote is checked

Every source is downloaded and read as text. A script checks every quote, all 64 of them, against that text word for word, and checks that a quote with one word changed is not found, so the check is known to be able to fail. Every claim that a platform says nothing on an issue is checked the same way: none of the issue's words may appear in the platform. That check was added after a draft summary wrongly said the national Republican platform did not otherwise address the environment.

What is missing

  • The African Methodist Episcopal Church: no General Conference resolution or Council of Bishops statement was found on any issue except immigration. Its website offers no searchable archive.
  • The Assemblies of God: its position papers cover immigration and abortion only. Two papers it refers to, on capital punishment and on environmental protection, are not published.

Qualifying documents not yet read:

  • African Methodist Episcopal Church: AME General Conference resolutions, which outrank the Council of Bishops. None found online; the church paper's summary of the 2024 General Conference legislation names none on immigration.

What these pages do not do

They give no party and no church a score or a ranking, on the page, on a share card or in the data. The one count on these pages is on the Texas House page: on bills the Texas Catholic bishops themselves took a side on, how often each party’s majority voted their way. It covers that one church, is never combined with another, and is not a verdict on which party is “more Christian”. The pages do not interpret scripture. Where traditions disagree with each other, the pages show the disagreement. And members of every tradition hold a range of views.
