John Bucy, Texas House District 136

John Bucy represents Texas House District 136 and is a Democrat. This page is the record: of the 67 votes this site publishes, John Bucy cast 66, and on the 50 of those where the two parties took opposite sides, they voted against their own party 4 times.

Where they broke with their own party

  • HB 5111 — Sets who may serve as a campaign treasurer: at least 18, a Texas resident, not already treasurer for someone else, and not convicted of certain election offences. history
  • HB 121 — Lets the Texas Education Agency commission its own police officers, requires a district claiming a good cause exception from the armed security requirement to adopt an alternative standard such as a school marshal, and changes who sits on the state’s School Safety Center board. history
  • HB 23 — Lets an applicant have a licensed engineer review or inspect development documents, instead of waiting for the local government to do it. history
  • HB 3892 — Stops counties from limiting how many homes can be built per acre outside city limits, including through minimum lot sizes, lot widths or setbacks. history

How they voted on the bills people have heard of

These seven drew the most attention of the 67 this site publishes. Every member has a position on record for each one, whichever way they went.

  • SB 2 Education savings accounts Voted against
    Creates state-funded accounts that families can spend on private school tuition and other approved school costs. The money comes from the state budget. history
  • SB 8 Immigration enforcement Voted against
    Requires the sheriff of every county that runs a jail, or contracts one out, to request an agreement letting their deputies enforce federal immigration law, and to ask again each year if no agreement is reached. Creates state grants, sized by the county’s population, to pay for it. history
  • SB 10 Ten Commandments in classrooms Voted against
    Requires every public school classroom in Texas to display a copy of the Ten Commandments. history
  • SB 3 THC ban Voted against
    Bans hemp products containing THC — the ones sold in smoke shops and convenience stores — and adds licences, fees and criminal penalties for selling them. history
  • SB 14 Regulatory review Voted against
    Creates a state office that reviews agency rules and recommends repealing ones it finds unnecessary. It also tells judges to decide for themselves what a law means, rather than accepting the agency’s reading. history
  • SB 6 Electric grid and large power users Voted for
    Sets terms for power users above 75 megawatts at one site, such as data centres. They must help pay to connect, and can be ordered to cut usage during an energy emergency alert. history
  • SB 5 Dementia research institute Voted for
    Creates a state institute that awards grants for dementia, Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s research and prevention. It takes effect only if voters approve a constitutional amendment setting up the institute and its fund. history
