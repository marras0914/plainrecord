Ken King, Texas House District 88

Ken King represented Texas House District 88 in the 89th Legislature (the 2025 session) and is a Republican. This page is the record: of the 67 votes this site publishes, Ken King cast 51, and on the 39 of those where the two parties took opposite sides, they voted against their own party 20 times.

Where they broke with their own party

  • HB 5616 — Creates a state program and fund to promote the presidential libraries in Texas as historical and tourist attractions. history
  • HB 2284 — Requires music therapists to hold a state licence before they can practise, and allows the state to charge fees for it. history
  • HB 3883 — Makes an FIA World Endurance Championship car race eligible for state event-reimbursement money. history
  • HB 2446 — For medical bills of $50,000 or less, removes the need for a sworn affidavit to prove the charges were reasonable and the care was necessary. history
  • HB 2341 — Makes time credit automatic, rather than up to the judge, for state jail inmates who take part in an education, treatment or work programme. history
  • SB 1498 — Lets the state seize cryptocurrency through civil forfeiture, by adding digital currency to the property that can be taken as suspected proceeds of a crime. history
  • HB 4443 — Creates a pilot programme in Dallas-Fort Worth in which the state, community colleges and employers train workers for jobs in energy-efficient technology. history
  • HB 2442 — Makes one mail-in ballot application cover both the election and any runoff, unless the voter asks not to be sent the runoff ballot. history
  • HB 5111 — Sets who may serve as a campaign treasurer: at least 18, a Texas resident, not already treasurer for someone else, and not convicted of certain election offences. history
  • HB 1203 — Creates an office inside the Department of Agriculture responsible for keeping the state's food supply secure and free of disruption. history
  • HJR 218 — Asks voters to change the constitution so money from the Texas energy fund can pay for energy-efficiency projects that benefit electricity customers. history
  • HB 2234 — Raises the age at which someone is tried as an adult from 17 to 18, so 17-year-olds go through the juvenile system instead. It applies only to conduct on or after 1 September 2027. history
  • HB 5525 — Requires the parole board to write separate guidelines for people convicted of crimes they committed before turning 18, weighing rehabilitation and education. history
  • HB 510 — Requires the state to give a homeless person a free certified copy of their birth certificate when they ask for one. history
  • HB 636 — Requires the state housing agency to build a statewide network for sharing real-time data on homelessness. history
  • HB 2308 — Creates an 11-member advisory commission of state officials and legislators to study border security and illegal immigration. history
  • SB 1563 — Requires the state to create training for county jailers on dealing with veterans in custody. history
  • HB 2186 — Requires elevator mechanics, apprentices and contractors to register with the state before working on elevators. history
  • SB 973 — Lets appraisal districts put more property images online, including street-level exterior photos and aerial shots showing more than one building. history
  • HB 2298 — Creates a state grant programme to help Texas hospitals and clinics use artificial intelligence to scan medical images for cancer. history
