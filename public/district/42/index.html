Richard Raymond, Texas House District 42

Richard Raymond represents Texas House District 42 and is a Democrat. This page is the record: of the 67 votes this site publishes, Richard Raymond cast 67, and on the 51 of those where the two parties took opposite sides, they voted against their own party 16 times.

Where they broke with their own party

  • SB 1233 — Requires the state to produce information about perinatal palliative care, and requires a doctor to give it to a pregnant woman when an unborn child is diagnosed with a life-limiting condition. A doctor who does not can face an administrative penalty. history
  • HB 5138 — Lets the attorney general prosecute election-law crimes when the local prosecutor has not acted within six months of receiving a police report. history
  • SB 33 — Bars cities and counties from spending public money or giving logistical support to abortion providers, or to groups that help people obtain an abortion. history
  • SB 2185 — Requires school districts excused from running a bilingual programme to report which alternative language methods they use, so those methods count towards bilingual education funding. history
  • SB 1362 — Bars Texas courts and agencies from recognising or enforcing red-flag orders that remove firearms from someone judged a risk, and makes doing so a crime. history
  • HB 1128 — Lets an election judge with a handgun licence, or an early voting clerk serving as one, carry a concealed handgun at a polling place while on duty. Doing so is otherwise an offence. history
  • SB 17 — Bars people and companies tied to certain foreign governments, China among them, from buying land or property in Texas, and makes doing so a crime. history
  • HB 5246 — Reorganises the state's space agencies, widens the aerospace office's remit from space to aviation as well, and abolishes the spaceport trust fund. history
  • HB 30 — Lets a local government in a declared disaster area use a different formula for the tax rate it can set without voter approval, based on its disaster relief costs. history
  • HB 121 — Lets the Texas Education Agency commission its own police officers, requires a district claiming a good cause exception from the armed security requirement to adopt an alternative standard such as a school marshal, and changes who sits on the state’s School Safety Center board. history
  • HB 229 — Writes definitions of male and female into state law and requires government records that collect sex to use them. history
  • HB 23 — Lets an applicant have a licensed engineer review or inspect development documents, instead of waiting for the local government to do it. history
  • HB 3892 — Stops counties from limiting how many homes can be built per acre outside city limits, including through minimum lot sizes, lot widths or setbacks. history
  • SB 8 — Requires the sheriff of every county that runs a jail, or contracts one out, to request an agreement letting their deputies enforce federal immigration law, and to ask again each year if no agreement is reached. Creates state grants, sized by the county’s population, to pay for it. history
  • SB 10 — Requires every public school classroom in Texas to display a copy of the Ten Commandments. history
  • SB 14 — Creates a state office that reviews agency rules and recommends repealing ones it finds unnecessary. It also tells judges to decide for themselves what a law means, rather than accepting the agency’s reading. history

How they voted on the bills people have heard of

These seven drew the most attention of the 67 this site publishes. Every member has a position on record for each one, whichever way they went.

  • SB 2 Education savings accounts Voted against
    Creates state-funded accounts that families can spend on private school tuition and other approved school costs. The money comes from the state budget. history
  • SB 8 Immigration enforcement Voted for
    Requires the sheriff of every county that runs a jail, or contracts one out, to request an agreement letting their deputies enforce federal immigration law, and to ask again each year if no agreement is reached. Creates state grants, sized by the county’s population, to pay for it. history
  • SB 10 Ten Commandments in classrooms Voted for
    Requires every public school classroom in Texas to display a copy of the Ten Commandments. history
  • SB 3 THC ban Voted against
    Bans hemp products containing THC — the ones sold in smoke shops and convenience stores — and adds licences, fees and criminal penalties for selling them. history
  • SB 14 Regulatory review Voted for
    Creates a state office that reviews agency rules and recommends repealing ones it finds unnecessary. It also tells judges to decide for themselves what a law means, rather than accepting the agency’s reading. history
  • SB 6 Electric grid and large power users Voted for
    Sets terms for power users above 75 megawatts at one site, such as data centres. They must help pay to connect, and can be ordered to cut usage during an energy emergency alert. history
  • SB 5 Dementia research institute Voted for
    Creates a state institute that awards grants for dementia, Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s research and prevention. It takes effect only if voters approve a constitutional amendment setting up the institute and its fund. history
