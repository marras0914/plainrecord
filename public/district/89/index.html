Candy Noble, Texas House District 89

Candy Noble represents Texas House District 89 and is a Republican. This page is the record: of the 67 votes this site publishes, Candy Noble cast 67, and on the 51 of those where the two parties took opposite sides, they voted against their own party 13 times.

Where they broke with their own party

  • HB 2284 — Requires music therapists to hold a state licence before they can practise, and allows the state to charge fees for it. history
  • SB 1498 — Lets the state seize cryptocurrency through civil forfeiture, by adding digital currency to the property that can be taken as suspected proceeds of a crime. history
  • HB 2442 — Makes one mail-in ballot application cover both the election and any runoff, unless the voter asks not to be sent the runoff ballot. history
  • HB 4838 — Requires the state to hire a language access coordinator and find where people who do not speak English cannot reach health and social services or 2-1-1. history
  • HB 510 — Requires the state to give a homeless person a free certified copy of their birth certificate when they ask for one. history
  • HB 2308 — Creates an 11-member advisory commission of state officials and legislators to study border security and illegal immigration. history
  • HB 2186 — Requires elevator mechanics, apprentices and contractors to register with the state before working on elevators. history
  • HB 1784 — Extends the help colleges must give students who were in foster care — including a named staff liaison — to students who are homeless. history
  • HB 871 — Updates the building codes Texas cities must use for homes and commercial buildings from the 2012 editions to the 2018 editions. history
  • HB 3892 — Stops counties from limiting how many homes can be built per acre outside city limits, including through minimum lot sizes, lot widths or setbacks. history
  • SB 973 — Lets appraisal districts put more property images online, including street-level exterior photos and aerial shots showing more than one building. history
  • HB 2298 — Creates a state grant programme to help Texas hospitals and clinics use artificial intelligence to scan medical images for cancer. history
  • HB 3947 — Requires regional transport planners to take account of the travel needs of people who use state health, welfare and workforce services. history

How they voted on the bills people have heard of

These seven drew the most attention of the 67 this site publishes. Every member has a position on record for each one, whichever way they went.

  • SB 2 Education savings accounts Voted for
    Creates state-funded accounts that families can spend on private school tuition and other approved school costs. The money comes from the state budget. history
  • SB 8 Immigration enforcement Voted for
    Requires the sheriff of every county that runs a jail, or contracts one out, to request an agreement letting their deputies enforce federal immigration law, and to ask again each year if no agreement is reached. Creates state grants, sized by the county’s population, to pay for it. history
  • SB 10 Ten Commandments in classrooms Voted for
    Requires every public school classroom in Texas to display a copy of the Ten Commandments. history
  • SB 3 THC ban Voted for
    Bans hemp products containing THC — the ones sold in smoke shops and convenience stores — and adds licences, fees and criminal penalties for selling them. history
  • SB 14 Regulatory review Voted for
    Creates a state office that reviews agency rules and recommends repealing ones it finds unnecessary. It also tells judges to decide for themselves what a law means, rather than accepting the agency’s reading. history
  • SB 6 Electric grid and large power users Voted for
    Sets terms for power users above 75 megawatts at one site, such as data centres. They must help pay to connect, and can be ordered to cut usage during an energy emergency alert. history
  • SB 5 Dementia research institute Voted for
    Creates a state institute that awards grants for dementia, Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s research and prevention. It takes effect only if voters approve a constitutional amendment setting up the institute and its fund. history
