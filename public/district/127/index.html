Charles Cunningham, Texas House District 127

Charles Cunningham represented Texas House District 127 in the 89th Legislature (the 2025 session) and is a Republican. This page is the record: of the 67 votes this site publishes, Charles Cunningham cast 65, and on the 50 of those where the two parties took opposite sides, they voted against their own party 12 times.

Where District 127 is

District 127 is in Harris County, and 202,964 people lived in it at the 2020 Census.

  • Houston 37% of the district · 3% of Houston’s residents
  • Atascocita 34% of the district · 78% of Atascocita’s residents · unincorporated community
  • Spring 9% of the district · 29% of Spring’s residents · unincorporated community
  • Humble 2% of the district · 19% of Humble’s residents

The other 19% of the district lives outside any city or named community.

Where they broke with their own party

  • HB 4811 — Makes South by Southwest eligible for state event-reimbursement money, and exempts it from having to show it considered holding the festival outside Texas. history
  • HB 3335 — Lets a county donate surplus property to a civic or charitable group without first showing that an auction would fail, but requires it instead to find that the county can keep enough control to ensure the donation serves its public purpose. history
  • HB 2341 — Makes time credit automatic, rather than up to the judge, for state jail inmates who take part in an education, treatment or work programme. history
  • HB 4443 — Creates a pilot programme in Dallas-Fort Worth in which the state, community colleges and employers train workers for jobs in energy-efficient technology. history
  • HB 2442 — Makes one mail-in ballot application cover both the election and any runoff, unless the voter asks not to be sent the runoff ballot. history
  • HB 5111 — Sets who may serve as a campaign treasurer: at least 18, a Texas resident, not already treasurer for someone else, and not convicted of certain election offences. history
  • HB 2234 — Raises the age at which someone is tried as an adult from 17 to 18, so 17-year-olds go through the juvenile system instead. It applies only to conduct on or after 1 September 2027. history
  • HB 510 — Requires the state to give a homeless person a free certified copy of their birth certificate when they ask for one. history
  • HB 636 — Requires the state housing agency to build a statewide network for sharing real-time data on homelessness. history
  • HB 2186 — Requires elevator mechanics, apprentices and contractors to register with the state before working on elevators. history
  • HB 3179 — Lets the county where the Llano and James rivers meet charge its own hotel tax, except at hotels a city already taxes. history
  • SB 973 — Lets appraisal districts put more property images online, including street-level exterior photos and aerial shots showing more than one building. history
